    Steering Control Dynamics: Signal Chain, Design Rationale and Known Defects¶

    This document describes how Gripora turns the driver's steering and throttle into steering-servo and ESC commands. It records why each stage is built the way it is, and lists the known defects and limitations. Read it before changing steering-control code: src/main/flight/{setpoint,pid,drive,drive_aids,speed_atten,mixer,servos,motors}.c, src/main/fc/rc.c, src/main/fc/core.c and src/main/flight/failsafe.c.

    The page keeps its old address (contributing/tech/flight-dynamics/) because source comments and the firmware's AGENTS.md link to it. Directory and file names such as src/main/flight/ are inherited from the fixed-wing firmware Gripora was forked from (WingFlight).

    Scope. Everything between the receiver and the servo/motor outputs: RC processing, setpoint shaping, the steering rate PID, the drive state, speed and throttle gain attenuation, GYRO OFF, the drive aids (ABS and traction control), the mixer, servo and motor output, and the arming and failsafe logic that overrides them.

    Basis. A static read of the firmware source at the time of writing, checked against the unit tests where they exist. Nothing here was verified by driving. Where a statement depends on behaviour that can only be observed on a car, it says so.

    1. Conventions¶

    These are easy to get wrong.

    Item Convention Where
    Axes Only yaw (steering) is stabilised. Roll and pitch keep their slots in every per-axis array, PID and rate profile, MSP message and Blackbox field, but their setpoints and PID outputs are always zero. setpointUpdate() zeroes roll/pitch setpoints; pidResetRollPitch() in pid.c
    Yaw sign RC yaw (steering stick) is clockwise-positive; gyro yaw is negative for the same motion. setpoint.c negates the RC yaw once, and everything downstream keeps that sign. GYRO OFF negates the raw channel the same way, so the steering direction is the same with and without the gyro. setpointUpdate(), mixerUpdateInputs()
    Pitch sign attitude.values.pitch / attitude.raw[PITCH] are positive nose-down (inherited Betaflight convention). Nothing in the steering loop uses pitch; it matters for attitude telemetry and the Blackbox attitude field. imu.c
    Throttle Signed, -1..1 around rc_center: forward above, brake/reverse below. Inputs within 10 µs of centre are neutral (RC_THROTTLE_NEUTRAL_DEADBAND). "Throttle off" for arming, auto-disarm and failsafe is the stick within 15 µs of centre (isThrottleOff(): that deadband plus RC_THROTTLE_DEADBAND, 5 µs). fc/rc.c
    Channel order Default map RT1234AE: CH1 steering, CH2 throttle, CH3-CH6 AUX1-4. Roll and pitch are unused and parked on CH7/CH8. pg/rx.c (rcmap)
    PID output The PID sum is a unitless steering command: ±1.0 is the full stabilized-yaw mixer input, which the default rule sends to S1 at weight 1000, i.e. full servo scale. pidSum into MIXER_IN_STABILIZED_YAW; limits ±1000 in pg/mixer.c
    Units Setpoint and gyro rate in °/s. axisError (the I accumulator) in degrees, limited by error_limit (degrees). Drive speeds in km/h (settings in 0.1 km/h). pid.c, drive.c
    Loop timing pid.dT comes from gyro.targetLooptime. With pid_process_denom > 1, taskMainPidLoop() in core.c spreads setpoint → PID → mixer → servo across consecutive ticks. activePidLoopDenom cases

    Signal chain¶

     RX (rx.c) ─► rcInput ─► rc.c: deadbands, ±1 deflection; throttle signed -1..1 around rc_center
                              │
                              ▼
        setpoint.c: yaw sign ─► smoothing ─► yaw dynamic deadband/ceiling
                    ─► response/accel limit (sp.deflection) ─► boost ─► rates curve (sp.setpoint)
                    roll and pitch setpoints forced to 0
                              │
                              ▼
        pid.c: driveUpdate() + speedAttenUpdate(), yaw I cleared on a direction change
               yaw gyro × (yaw_gyro_reverse ? -1 : 1) × travel direction (±1)
               P, D: Kp/Kd · masterGain · gainCurve · atten · corr
               I:    Ki · masterGain · corr · axisError
               F, B: Kf/Kb · atten            (atten = max(TPA · SPA, 0.25), corr = drive correction scale)
                              │
                              ▼
        mixer.c inputs: STABILIZED_YAW = pidSum   (GYRO OFF: raw steering channel instead)
                        STABILIZED_THROTTLE = driveAidsApply(throttle)   (failsafe: failsafe_throttle instead)
                              │
                        rule table (default: STABILIZED_YAW → S1, STABILIZED_THROTTLE → M1)
                              │
              servos.c (curve, speed, reverse, scale, trim, travel)      motors.c (bidirectional: 0 = neutral)

    drive.c reads the stabilized throttle the mixer sent on the previous loop (failsafe included) as its command input, so the drive state follows what the ESC is actually told to do.

    2. Subsystems: what they do and why¶

    2.1 RC processing — fc/rc.c¶

    Each stick is centred on rc_center, a per-axis deadband is removed (roll_deadband, pitch_deadband, yaw_deadband; the throttle uses a fixed 10 µs), and the result is scaled to ±1 by rc_deflection. The throttle is that same signed deflection: there is no 0..1 throttle any more. getThrottle() returns it; the Blackbox logs it x1000 as rcCommand[4]. AUX channels are passed on as rcInput − rc_center.

    2.2 Setpoint shaping — flight/setpoint.c¶

    Order of operations: yaw negation, smoothing (cutoff derived from the measured RX frame interval), yaw dynamic deadband/ceiling, response-time and acceleration limit (→ sp.deflection), setpoint boost, rates curve (→ sp.setpoint). Roll and pitch are computed and then set to zero; their stick deflection is still available to mixer rules through the RC inputs.

    • Smoothing tied to the RX frame rate. Inherited from Rotorflight: it removes stair-stepping from slow link updates without a fixed lag.
    • Rates. rcRates × 5 °/s at full stick on the default curve. The default yaw rate is 150 °/s (pg/rates.c); the Configurator's setup wizard driving styles set 90 (Beginner), 150 (Sport) or 300 °/s (Drift).
    • Yaw dynamic deadband is on by default (yaw_dynamic_deadband_gain 10), inherited.

    2.3 Steering rate PID — flight/pid.c¶

    error = setpoint − gyroRate, on yaw only (pid_mode 1; any value other than 0 runs mode 1). Before the error is formed, the yaw gyro rate is negated if yaw_gyro_reverse is on, and multiplied by the travel direction from the drive state (§2.4). Terms:

    Term Formula Notes
    P Kp · masterGain · gainCurve · atten · corr · error
    I Ki · masterGain · corr · axisError Not attenuated by TPA or SPA. Accumulation is also scaled by corr.
    D Kd · masterGain · gainCurve · atten · corr · d/dt(−gyro) Gyro-only D (no setpoint kick). Default D = 0.
    F Kf · atten · setpoint Carries the steady stick response. Not scaled by corr, so the stick always steers.
    B Kb · atten · d/dt(setpoint) Feedforward boost. Not scaled by corr.

    atten = max(TPA × SPA, 0.25) (PID_ATTENUATION_MIN); corr is driveGetCorrectionScale() (§2.4).

    Default authority (defaults from pg/pid.c, scale constants from flight/pid.h): yaw P 190, I 45, D 0, F 65, B 35, error_limit 60°.

    Quantity Value
    P at 100 °/s error 190 × 6.67e-6 × 100 ≈ 0.13 of full travel
    I ceiling (Ki · error_limit) 45 × 2e-4 × 60 = 0.54 of full travel
    F per °/s 65 × 2.5e-5 = 0.0016, so 615 °/s of setpoint is full travel from F alone
    F at full stick, default 150 °/s ≈ 0.24 of full travel

    Design decisions:

    • Gyro reversed for reverse travel, stick unchanged. The yaw response to a steering angle is proportional to the signed speed (yaw rate ≈ v · tan(steer) / wheelbase), so reversing flips its sign and a forward tune becomes positive feedback. The stick keeps meaning "wheel angle" in both directions, so correcting in reverse is exactly negating the gyro rate the loop sees: P, I and D all follow, F and B are untouched (pid.c pidApplyGyroRate(), drive.c header comment).
    • yaw_gyro_reverse (PID config PG v4) negates only this loop's yaw gyro, for when the gyro corrects the wrong way (typically a reversed steering channel fixed with a servo reverse). Board orientation and the attitude estimate are unchanged. It is in MSP_MIXER_CONFIG as an optional trailing byte.
    • Correction scale on P, I and D only. At a standstill there is no yaw response to correct with, so P and D would only push the steering and I would wind up; F and B carry the stick and must always steer the wheels.
    • I-term cleared on a direction change, because it was accumulated for the other sign of the plant.
    • I held at zero while disarmed (yaw only): on the bench the car cannot respond, and whatever built up would arrive as a steering bump on arming. P and D still act while disarmed so the setup wizard's gyro-direction check (turn the car by hand, the wheels steer against it) works.
    • Throttle gain attenuation (fw_tpa_gain, fw_tpa_curve). A baseline scale times an optional curve from the shared gain-curve pool, evaluated on |throttle| (0..1), mirroring master_gain + gain_curve. Applied to P, D, F and B, not I.
    • Speed attenuation (fw_spa_gain, fw_spa_curve, fw_spa_speed_max), flight/speed_atten.c. A car's yaw response to a steering angle grows with speed, so a tune that is right at moderate speed can oscillate flat out; TPA cannot see that when the throttle is closed at speed. Same shape as TPA, with the curve evaluated at speed / fw_spa_speed_max (default 60 km/h, range 10-300). Multiplied with TPA.
      • Speed source: the RPM wheel speed from the drive state when there is an RPM source, otherwise GPS (max(speed3d, groundSpeed); only u-blox reports 3D speed).
      • Filtering: low-passed at 1 Hz (FW_SPA_SPEED_CUTOFF_HZ).
      • Speed loss (no RPM source and no GPS fix): hold the last scale for 3 s, then slew back to 1.0 at 0.2/s. Engaging (first speed, speed regained, profile change) slews onto the curve at the same rate.
      • With no curve SPA is exactly 1.0. Per PID profile, in PG_FW_SPA_CONFIG (v1). Unit-tested in speed_atten_unittest.
    • Master gain applied at the point of use, not baked into coef[], so live adjustments stay correct whichever adjustment last touched a coefficient.
    • I-term relax (iterm_relax, the "bounceback" score 1-10, default 5, and iterm_relax_level, default 22 °/s): a high-pass of the setpoint scales I accumulation down during fast steering input. pidItermRelaxCutoff() maps the score to the setpoint low-pass cutoff (50, 30, 20, 15, 10, 8, 7, 6, 5, 3 Hz). Always on.
    • I-term decay (iterm_decay_time, default 60 = 0.6 s time constant, range 1-100; iterm_decay_limit, default 35 °/s): I bleeds continuously, so a stale I-term does not hold the steering off centre.
    • Anti-windup: accumulation stops only when the stabilized-yaw mixer input is saturated and the error would push further into saturation (pidAxisSaturated()). Servo travel clipping also raises saturation (mixerSaturateServoOutput()), so trim-induced clipping is covered. Saturation flags last MIXER_SATURATION_TIME (5) ticks.
    • I held in GYRO OFF. While GYRO OFF drives the steering (mixerStabilizationBypassed(), false once failsafe is active) the gyro is not tracking the setpoint, so I is not integrated; decay still runs.
    • Gyro overflow (gyroOverflowDetected()): pidReset() zeroes all PID state and outputs.

    Mode 0 (pid_mode = 0) outputs F only: a stick-proportional command with no gyro.

    2.4 Drive state — flight/drive.c, PG_DRIVE_CONFIG¶

    The drive state estimates the travel direction and the wheel speed for the steering loop and the drive aids.

    • Inputs. The stabilized throttle sent to the ESC (failsafe included), and the motor RPM when motor 1 has an RPM source (DShot telemetry, ESC telemetry or a frequency sensor), averaged with motor 2 if it has one too. RPM is a magnitude: no source reports a direction, so the direction comes from what the ESC was commanded to do. Wheel speed = motor RPM ÷ motor1_gear_ratio × π × drive_wheel_diameter, filtered at 10 Hz. Stopped = below 1 km/h for 0.1 s.
    • ESC model, drive_esc_mode. The commanded state (neutral, forward, brake, reverse) follows the ESC's own rules. A command within 5 % of neutral is neutral.
      • FWD_BRK_REV: below neutral brakes after forward; back to neutral (held at least 50 ms, DRIVE_NEUTRAL_DWELL_S), then below again, reverses. A brake or reverse carries on while the stick stays below neutral. Reverse is also the first below-neutral command after arming.
      • FWD_REV: below neutral reverses, braking or coasting first while RPM shows the car still rolling forward.
      • FWD_BRK: below neutral only ever brakes.
      • AUTO: FWD_REV on a DShot protocol (the AM32 default), FWD_BRK_REV otherwise. An AM32 with "RC car reverse" on needs FWD_BRK_REV set by hand.
    • Direction with RPM changes only at zero speed: an RPM-confirmed standstill, or the moment the wheels pass through zero (the unfiltered speed shows it). Braking while rolling forward is never taken for reverse. Wheels speeding up from zero under a below-neutral command are taken as reverse in every mode that can reverse, and under an above-neutral command as forward, so a reversal the model did not expect (a sensored ESC, an AM32 reversing at its own threshold) is still caught.
    • Direction without RPM follows the command at once (forward +1, reverse −1; brake and neutral keep it). The ESC may brake or coast for a while before it really changes direction, so on each change the correction scale drops to zero and fades back in over 1 s (DRIVE_DIR_CHANGE_RAMP_S).
    • Correction scale = speed factor × reverse factor × ramp factor:
      • speed factor (RPM only): 0 below drive_gyro_min_speed (default 2.0 km/h, never below 1.0 km/h, the stop speed, so the correction is faded out before the direction can flip), rising linearly to 1 at drive_gyro_full_speed (default 6.0 km/h, always above the minimum). Without RPM it is 1.
      • reverse factor: 1 forward; in reverse drive_gyro_reverse_gain % (default 50), or 0 with drive_gyro_reverse OFF.
      • ramp factor: the 1 s fade after an unconfirmed direction change.
    • Disarmed the state is held at neutral and forward, the ramp is complete and the speed factor is not applied, so the gyro corrects on the bench (with I held at zero, §2.3). Every arming starts from neutral and forward.
    • Debug mode DRIVE: throttle command x1000, commanded state, direction, speed x10, speed known, correction scale x1000, motor 1 RPM, stopped. Unit-tested in drive_unittest.

    2.5 GYRO OFF — flight/mixer.c¶

    BOXPASSTHROUGH, shown as GYRO OFF (permanent id 12). While it is on and failsafe is not active, the stabilized-yaw mixer input is replaced by the steering channel's deflection, negated to keep the stabilized sign (§1): no rates, no expo, no PID. It is written directly (mixerSetBypassInput()), so the stabilized-yaw input's min/max limits do not apply; the input rate and the servo's own limits still do. While disarmed, a bench override or a wiggle still wins over the switch.

    Only the steering is bypassed. The throttle still goes through the drive aids (§2.6); DRIVE AIDS OFF is the separate switch for those.

    2.6 Drive aids (ABS and traction control) — flight/drive_aids.c, PG_DRIVE_AIDS_CONFIG¶

    The driver's throttle/brake command passes through driveAidsApply() before it becomes STABILIZED_THROTTLE. An aid only ever moves the command towards neutral, never past it and never into the opposite direction. Failsafe replaces the result afterwards, so a failsafe throttle is never shaped. The aids pass the command through unchanged while disarmed, in failsafe and with DRIVE AIDS OFF (permanent id 66). A mixer rule fed from the RC throttle input instead of STABILIZED_THROTTLE bypasses them. Every aid is OFF by default. The settings are per PID profile (copied and reset with the profile).

    • ABS (timed). Runs only while the drive state has the ESC braking (state BRAKE) and the car is not RPM-confirmed stopped, and the brake is past drive_abs_trigger. Each cycle (1 / drive_abs_freq) holds the brake command for half the cycle plus drive_abs_hold (at most 85 % of the cycle), then eases it to drive_abs_floor % of the command. DYNAMIC moves the rate towards drive_abs_dyn_freq, the floor towards 60 % of drive_abs_floor and the hold towards drive_abs_dyn_hold as the brake deepens from the trigger to full. drive_abs_corner_freq adds rate and drive_abs_corner_hold moves the hold with steering-stick deflection; where both a dynamic and a steering hold apply, the shorter wins. The rate is limited to 2-33 Hz.
    • 10 % minimum release. A brake-then-reverse ESC that sees neutral in the middle of a brake reverses on the next pull, so the released brake never drops below 10 % brake (DRIVE_ABS_MIN_BRAKE). The command stays on the brake side of neutral, and the drive state stays BRAKE with it.
    • Traction control (RAMP). A forward command may only rise at a limited rate, zero to full in drive_tc_ramp_time (50-2000 ms, default 500); letting off is instant. It shapes only a forward command, never a brake (including a command above neutral while the ESC brakes a car rolling backwards) or reverse. While it, or the aids, are off the limit follows the command, so turning it on with the trigger held never cuts the throttle.
    • Timing only. Both use the sticks and time, no wheel-slip sensing.
    • Debug mode DRIVE_AIDS: throttle in and out x1000, flags, ABS rate x10 (or TC limit x1000 when driving forward with TC on), floor x1000, hold ms, cycle phase ms, steering x1000. Unit-tested in drive_aids_unittest.

    2.7 Mixer — flight/mixer.c, pg/mixer.c¶

    A rule table: output (SET/ADD/MUL) = (offset + weight · curve(input · rate)) / 1000, with a separate negative weight, an optional slew (speed) and an optional logic-condition gate. model_type is CAR (0) or CUSTOM (1). The default (car) rules are SET STABILIZED_YAW → S1 (steering) and SET STABILIZED_THROTTLE → M1 (reversible ESC); rear steering and a second ESC are added by the Configurator's car preset as further rules. The stabilized throttle input range is −1000..1000.

    • Saturation flags last 5 ticks and gate the PID's anti-windup.
    • Overrides and wiggle apply only while disarmed. The wiggle (ready, armed, arming-error patterns, wiggle_* settings) moves only the steering (yaw) input (flight/wiggle.c).
    • Non-finite guard. A NaN or infinity at a mixer input, a rule output or an accumulated output is replaced by zero (and counted as saturation at the input).
    • Live-adjustable rule weights keep the configured polarity in mixerRuleSign[], because a live magnitude passes through 0, which has no sign.

    2.8 Servo output — flight/servos.c¶

    Order: mixer output (or a bench override) → geometry correction → balance curve → non-finite guard → speed limit (each servo on its own) → reverse → rpos/rneg scale → trim (saved + runtime) → travel limit → mid.

    • There are 24 servos; servo N goes to PWM output N where the board has one and to channel N of an SBUS/F.Bus output, from one output stage. Every servo has the signal range 880-2159 µs and one PWM update rate (servo_pwm_rate).
    • Trim never edits mid, so min/max (offsets from mid) stay where they are. Saved and runtime trim are added before the travel limit, so the output stays inside min/max.

    2.9 Motor output — flight/motors.c, drivers/¶

    Every motor defaults to motor_control_mode bidirectional, with min_throttle 1000 and max_throttle 2000. A zero command to a bidirectional motor is neutral: on PWM, SRXL2 and SITL the midpoint of min_throttle..max_throttle, never min_command, which a car ESC reads as full brake/reverse and many learn as their neutral at power-up. On DShot zero is the motor-stop command, positive commands use the forward range and negative ones the reverse range.

    2.10 Attitude estimator — flight/imu.c¶

    The inherited Mahony quaternion filter still runs on all three axes (gyro integration corrected by the accelerometer, plus magnetometer or GPS course when available). Accelerometer readings are only used within 0.9-1.1 g; the gain is x10 while disarmed, with a quick re-convergence after a disarm. Nothing in the steering loop or the drive aids uses the attitude: it feeds the arming check (§2.11), telemetry and the Blackbox.

    2.11 Arming, running latch, failsafe — fc/core.c, flight/failsafe.c, rx/rx.c¶

    • Arming is blocked unless the throttle is at neutral (ARMING_DISABLED_THROTTLE) and the attitude estimate is established (isAttitudeEstimateReady(), which does not check that the car is level), among the other arming-disable flags. While armed with the throttle at neutral the armed beeper sounds; with stick arming, auto_disarm_delay (default 5 s) at neutral disarms.
    • Running latch (INFLIGHT_MODE, the telemetry RUNNING bit): set once armed and |throttle| > 0.1, held through stops, cleared on disarm. There is no airborne detection any more. A disarm while running, after at least rearm_min_armed_seconds (5 s) armed, opens a rearm_grace_seconds (10 s) window in which re-arming skips the throttle and attitude checks, so an accidental disarm can be recovered while the car is moving.
    • Receiver signal loss (rx.c): bad channels hold their last value for 300 ms, then each channel takes its rxfail value. The default (AUTO) puts roll, pitch and steering at centre and the throttle at neutral (getFailsafeThrottle() returns the throttle centre); other channels hold their last value.
    • Failsafe (failsafe.c) is monitoring from start-up and runs its state machine:
      • Armed with the throttle at neutral for at least failsafe_throttle_low_delay (10 s) before the link drops: disarm straight away ("just disarm").
      • Otherwise, after failsafe_delay (1.5 s) without a valid signal, failsafe_procedure runs: DROP (default) disarms at once; AUTO-LAND holds failsafe_throttle (default 1500 µs, neutral) for failsafe_off_delay (1 s), then disarms.
      • While a procedure is active, failsafe_throttle replaces the drive aids' output on the stabilized throttle, and GYRO OFF is ignored. Re-arming needs failsafe_recovery_delay (1 s) of valid signal.
      • A FAILSAFE mode switch acts as stage 1 (default), KILL (disarm at once) or STAGE2 (failsafe_switch_mode).

    3. Decision log (Gripora)¶

    Gripora was forked from WingFlight 0.0.43 in November 2026. The fixed-wing history (attitude hold, thrust vectoring, GPS navigation, the governor and so on) is in the WingFlight repository; none of that code remains.

    4. Known defects and limitations¶

    Confirmed = follows directly from the code. Needs verification = depends on behaviour that can only be observed on a car.

    High¶

    H-1. Without an RPM source there is no low-speed fade. Confirmed. The speed factor is 1 without RPM (driveSpeedFactor()), so armed at a standstill the full P and I correction runs. Holding steering off centre on a stopped car then makes P push and I wind up towards full lock, because the car cannot yaw at the commanded rate. The Configurator says so on the Motors tab. Fix: an RPM source (DShot telemetry, ESC telemetry or a frequency sensor).

    H-2. FWD_REV with no RPM: braking is taken for reverse, and ABS never runs. Confirmed. Without RPM, driveNextState() cannot know the car is still rolling, so every below-neutral command is REVERSE. The travel direction flips at once: the gyro rate is negated during what is physically still a forward brake, softened only by the 1 s correction fade-in, and the yaw I-term is cleared. ABS needs state BRAKE, so it never runs. The Configurator warns on the drive aids panel. Fixes: an RPM source, or FWD_BRK_REV if the ESC supports it.

    Medium¶

    M-1. The ESC model can disagree with the ESC. Needs verification. Without RPM the direction is only what the model predicts. FWD_BRK_REV reverses after 50 ms at neutral (DRIVE_NEUTRAL_DWELL_S); a real ESC may need a longer or shorter neutral, or have its own reverse threshold. With RPM, a reversal the model missed is caught when the wheels speed up from zero; without RPM it is not.

    M-2. Default steering authority from F is small. Confirmed arithmetic; needs verification on a car. With the defaults (yaw F 65, 150 °/s at full stick) feedforward gives about 24 % of full travel at full stick. With an RPM source and the car below drive_gyro_min_speed, P, I and D are scaled to zero, so F and the transient B are all the steering there is: an armed, stopped car may not reach full lock until it moves. Whether this matters in practice, and whether F or the default rate should change, has not been checked on a car.

    M-3. Wheel speed is driven-wheel speed. Inferred, not verified. Speed comes from motor RPM through the gear ratio, so it reads the driven wheels. Wheelspin or locked wheels under braking make it differ from ground speed, which affects the low-speed gyro fade, the "stopped" detection (which gates ABS) and speed attenuation.

    M-4. Drive aids act on the stick, not on the wheels. Confirmed (by design). ABS and traction control are timed from the stick and the clock. The ABS "steering" adjustments use the steering-stick deflection, not the actual wheel angle. There is no slip detection; sensor-based modes are planned as separate modes.

    Low¶

    • L-1. GYRO OFF bypasses the stabilized-yaw input limits. Confirmed, intentional. mixerSetBypassInput() writes the input directly, so a tightened mixer input min/max on stabilized yaw does not apply in GYRO OFF. Servo min/max still do.
    • L-2. GYRO OFF does not bypass the drive aids. Confirmed. Only the steering is raw; ABS and traction control still shape the throttle unless DRIVE AIDS OFF is on. User-facing text that says the throttle "passes straight" in GYRO OFF is describing the steering only.
    • L-3. Arming does not check that the car is level. Confirmed. isAttitudeEstimateReady() only checks that the attitude estimate is established. ARMING_DISABLED_ANGLE keeps its name because it is user-visible.
    • L-4. First IMU update integrates over a huge dt. Confirmed. previousIMUUpdateTime starts at 0, so the first step is long. Inherited from Betaflight; converges quickly, happens once per boot.
    • L-5. Accelerometer accepted at 0.9-1.1 g with no correction for cornering or acceleration. Confirmed. Only attitude telemetry and the arming check depend on it.

    Fixed in the inherited code and still in place¶

    • Failsafe state machine runs. The inherited Rotorflight code had failsafeStartMonitoring() commented out, so failsafe never ran; WingFlight re-enabled it, and it is called from core.c. (Older notes that say "stage 2 is disabled" are out of date.) There is still no running automated test (flight_failsafe_unittest.cc.txt).
    • Non-finite guard at the mixer inputs, rule outputs and the servo boundary.
    • Each servo is speed-limited on its own (the inherited helicopter code scaled all cyclic servos together).

    Not reviewed¶

    Dynamic notch and RPM filters, gyro/accelerometer drivers and calibration, logic_condition.c, Blackbox, MSP and CLI plumbing.

    5. Test coverage¶

    Running unit tests (make test, src/test/unit) cover the PID (pid_unittest), setpoint, curves, maths, speed attenuation, the drive state (drive_unittest), the drive aids (drive_aids_unittest), servos and telemetry status. The mixer, IMU, failsafe and arming-prevention tests exist but are disabled (flight_mixer_unittest.cc.txt, flight_imu_unittest.cc.txt, flight_failsafe_unittest.cc.txt, arming_prevention_unittest.cc.txt), so the mixer, failsafe and fc/core.c arming paths have no running tests.

    Worth adding, cheapest first:

    1. Drive state with the real ESC (M-1): Blackbox the DRIVE debug mode while braking, reversing and re-reversing with and without RPM, and compare the commanded state and direction with what the car did.
    2. Standstill steering (H-1, M-2): armed, stopped, full steering, with and without RPM; record mixer[2] and the servo output.
    3. Failsafe: revive flight_failsafe_unittest for the just-disarm, DROP and AUTO-LAND paths with a signed neutral throttle.
    4. Mixer: revive flight_mixer_unittest for GYRO OFF, the drive aids hand-off and the failsafe throttle replacement.
